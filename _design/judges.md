# JUDGE PANEL

## Lens winner: Granny: Classic-Escape — Five Days to Get Out
Scores:
- Granny: Classic-Escape — Five Days to Get Out: fun=8 feas=8 faith=10 TOTAL=26
- Granny: Pressure-Cooker: fun=9 feas=6 faith=7 TOTAL=22
- Granny: The Long Night — Systemic-Sandbox Loop: fun=9 feas=5 faith=9 TOTAL=23

Rationale: FAITHFULNESS (10): This is Granny, exactly. The real Granny is a 5-day survival loop with persistent lock/item progress across knockouts, sound-and-sight detection, a hide-in-wardrobe/under-bed mechanic, and a multi-lock front door as the canonical escape. Classic-Escape reproduces this skeleton beat-for-beat without inventing genre-foreign systems (hard real-time timers, secondary monsters, dynamic-music HUDs). A judge who cares about the source material sees this and immediately recognizes the game; the other two recognizably drift.\n\nFUN (8): The 8–12s LISTEN → PICK A LANE → ADVANCE → RESET micro-loop is the genre's proven heartbeat, and it earns its dread honestly — the through-wall low-pass cue (8000Hz same-room dropping to ~600Hz through a wall) turns every footstep into a question, which is the single most replicable source of tension here. The day-as-life structure gives the meta-arc real teeth ('escape in fewer days') and the persistent progress means a catch stings without wiping you, so the player always pushes forward. It loses a point to the other two on pure expressive ceiling — its solution space per obstacle is narrower than the sandbox's five-honest-answers promise, and it lacks the relentless adrenaline spike of a hard timer. But it is the most CONSISTENTLY fun across the widest skill range, which matters more than peak fun for a shipping product.\n\nFEASIBILITY (8): Highest of the three and this is decisive given a client 'furious about failed attempts.' It has NO hard real-time escalation schedule to choreograph, NO second AI agent (the Pressure-Cooker's 7:00 spider/raven and the sandbox's renderer-heavy stacked-furniture climbing are both schedule-killers), and a SINGLE escape route to fully validate instead of three balanced ones. Granny's Patrol/Search/Hunt state machine is the minimum viable AI, and the awareness meter with fill/decay is well-specified with numbers. The hard physics requirements (hinge doors, pushable barricades, collision-validated non-floating items) are scoped to exactly what's needed and no more. The riskiest items — assembled bolt cutter, carryable car battery that slows the player — are isolated set-pieces, not load-bearing systems.\n\nWHY NOT THE OTHERS: Pressure-Cooker has the best moment-to-moment design (the fractal speed-vs-noise decision, the squeeze/release rhythm, the dynamic-music tension HUD) but its 5-beat real-time escalation, blackout lighting transition, secondary roaming threat, AND three fully-balanced routes are roughly 2x the implementation surface — exactly where careless implementers ship broken. Its 8-minute hard timer is also the LEAST faithful choice; Granny has never been a stopwatch game, and a hard cutoff fights the genre's slow-dread identity. The Sandbox has the highest replay ceiling (emergent physics combos, puppeteering Granny with lures) but bets the entire experience on the two hardest-to-get-right systems in the stack: dynamic navmesh re-rasterization when player-pushed furniture blocks paths, and emergent furniture-stacking to reach windows. Both are precisely the cannon-es jank/pathing failures the client is enraged about. It's the most likely to be brilliant in a demo and broken in QA.

Graft:
- THROWN-ITEM NOISE LURES (from both Pressure-Cooker and Sandbox). This is the single most important graft. Classic-Escape's biggest weakness is a narrow solution space per obstacle — adding a throwable junk lure (right-click, ~8 m/s, creates a NoiseEvent at impact) gives the player a renewable tension-RELEASE verb and the puppeteering power-fantasy without any new AI. Granny already pathfinds to noise-event POSITIONS in Search state, so the lure costs almost nothing to add and roughly doubles expressive depth. Make Granny path to the noise POSITION (where you were), not your current position, to enable real misdirection.
- DYNAMIC MUSIC AS A TENSION HUD (from Pressure-Cooker). Classic-Escape relies heavily on raw footstep audio; layering 3–4 crossfaded procedural Web Audio layers driven by Granny's state (silent Patrol → cello swell when adjacent → pulse that speeds with awareness fill → chase staccato) converts dread from confusion into information. It's pure Web Audio, fits the no-binary-audio constraint, and is the cheapest single upgrade to perceived production quality.
- PROXIMITY HEARTBEAT THAT MASKS FOOTSTEPS (from Pressure-Cooker/Sandbox). A procedural heartbeat scaling 60→140 BPM with Granny's proximity manufactures the held-breath beat. The masking twist (heartbeat drowns her footsteps at <3m) is a genuinely clever sensory-overload mechanic that makes the close-call hide moment scarier — graft the masking, not just the heartbeat.
- THE FIDGET METER WHILE HIDDEN (from Pressure-Cooker). Classic-Escape's hide-spot has a wardrobe-peek anti-cheese but no hard cap on camping duration. A fidget meter that fills over ~12s and forces a cough/creak noise event prevents infinite hiding more elegantly than the 'stand still 40s triggers a creak' rule and is more diegetic.
- ANTI-SOFTLOCK TELEPORT-BACK RULE (from Pressure-Cooker's itemChain). Classic-Escape's carryable car battery and assembled bolt cutter are exactly the items a player can drop into a gap. Adopt the rule: any key item out of reach for 20s returns to its last valid rest spot. Cheap insurance against a run-ending bug.
- NAVMESH-EXCLUDES-FURNITURE + LOCAL STEERING (from Sandbox/Pressure-Cooker failureModes). Classic-Escape needs this discipline spelled out: build the navmesh on the static floorplan ONLY, treat pushed furniture as physics colliders Granny steers around, and on a stuck-detection (velocity <0.1 m/s for 1.5s while pathing) recompute or teleport her to her last valid node ONLY when off-screen and behind a wall.
- BARRICADE-BASHING AS AN AUDIBLE COUNTDOWN (from Sandbox). Classic-Escape already has barricades yielding in 6–9s; frame the shove-breaking as rhythmic, locatable bashing audio so it doubles as a 'how long do I have and where is she' signal. Pure presentation, big tension payoff.

Must fix:
- AUDIO LOCALIZATION IS THE WHOLE GAME — VALIDATE IT FIRST, BY EAR. The sound-first design collapses into guesswork if the 1/d falloff (clamped [1m..8m]) and the through-wall low-pass (8000Hz→600Hz) are even slightly wrong. Build a debug scene where Granny walks a fixed path and verify by ear that same-room vs through-wall vs through-two-walls are UNMISTAKABLE before building anything else. Keep the 1Hz grandfather-clock tick as a constant calibration anchor. If this fails, nothing else matters.
- ADD AN ACCESSIBILITY / BAD-SPEAKER FALLBACK FOR THE AUDIO THREAT CHANNEL. Localizing Granny purely by sound excludes hearing-impaired players and breaks entirely on laptop speakers. Add an optional, toggleable edge-of-screen directional pulse toward Granny when she's <8m, plus a 'last heard' marker. The Sandbox flagged this correctly; Classic-Escape omits it and must not ship without it.
- SPECIFY THE PHYSICS-STABILITY CONFIG EXPLICITLY (the client's #1 rage). The spec leans on hinge doors, pushable barricades, and a carryable battery but never pins the cannon-es settings. Lock these as named constants: fixed timestep 1/60 with up to 3 substeps, CCD on the player capsule and all thrown/small dynamic bodies, min collider thickness ≥0.1m, collision margin ~0.04m, clamp max body velocity ≤12 m/s, high contactEquationStiffness so the dresser doesn't sink, and sleep settled furniture to kill idle jitter. Never spawn bodies overlapping.
- HARDEN ITEM PLACEMENT VALIDATION TO PREVENT FLOATING/CLIPPED/UNREACHABLE ITEMS (the client's #2 rage). The '3 deterministic candidate slots per item' is good but the validation method is underspecified. Mandate: for each candidate anchor, raycast straight DOWN onto the surface collider and rest the item's lowest AABB point on the hit; REJECT any anchor whose downward ray or the item's AABB intersects another collider; REJECT any grab point >2.1m from floor on a non-walkable cell (reachability check for standing eye ~1.6m / reach ~2.1m, or crouch). Log a console error and fall back to the next candidate if validation fails. The carryable car battery especially must rest validly and be reachable.
- FIX SIGHT-RAYCAST FALSE POSITIVES/NEGATIVES. A single ray to the player's center will both clip a doorframe (missing a visible player) and shoot through a 1px wall gap (seeing through walls). Cast 3 rays (head/chest/hips), require ≥2 unobstructed for a sight hit, and ensure all static wall colliders are watertight — overlap adjacent panels by ~2cm so there are no sub-mm seams. This is unstated in Classic-Escape and will produce the 'she saw me through a wall' / 'she's staring right at me and doesn't react' complaints that read as broken.
- GUARANTEE THE FINAL 10s ALARM SPRINT IS ALWAYS WINNABLE FROM A LEGITIMATE STATE. Arming a 10s alarm with Granny already between the player and the <8m porch threshold is a guaranteed unfair loss. Enforce: the alarm summons Granny from her CURRENT position (never pre-teleported onto the threshold), the threshold is ≤8m from the door, and surface the optional shotgun stun as explicit insurance. Also make the Day-5 'she guards the door if Locks #1–2 are cleared' behavior leave at least one un-camped approach angle.
- MAKE PERSISTENT-PROGRESS STATE EXPLICIT ON RESPAWN. Players who assume a catch wipes their unlocks will rage-quit. On every respawn show a clear panel: 'Day 3 of 5 — Locks 1/3 cleared — Items: rusty key, screwdriver.' This is a one-screen UI addition that protects the entire meta-loop's readability.
- PREVENT THE SINGLE-SAFE-TILE CHEESE WITHOUT IT FEELING ARBITRARY. The 'stand still >40s with progress incomplete triggers a house creak that nudges Granny toward you' rule is good but tune it so it never fires during legitimate listening pauses (the 1–3s STOP & LISTEN beat is core). Gate the creak on cumulative stationary time AND no progress in the last ~60s, and make the creak diegetic/locatable so it reads as the house settling, not a punishment.

## Lens winner: Granny: Classic-Escape — Five Days to Get Out
Scores:
- Granny: Classic-Escape — Five Days to Get Out: fun=8 feas=9 faith=9 TOTAL=26
- Granny: Pressure-Cooker: fun=9 feas=7 faith=8 TOTAL=24
- Granny: The Long Night — Systemic-Sandbox Loop: fun=9 feas=7 faith=9 TOTAL=25

Rationale: The client's defining context is rage at repeated failed attempts. That makes feasibility the dominant axis, and the differentiator between these three is almost entirely scope, because they share the same movement/noise/sight numbers and the same hard physics requirements. Approach 1 is the only one that spends its entire build budget making the core trio correct — real rigid-body physics, deterministic collision-validated item placement, and a sound-first hearing+sight detection model — rather than splitting effort across features that are each their own bug surface.

Approach 1 (total 26): A single escape route with one linear, diegetically-hinted item->lock chain (rusty key -> screwdriver -> safe code -> bolt cutter -> battery -> keypad -> 3-lock door). One AI with a clean Patrol/Search/Hunt FSM. The 5-days-as-lives structure with preserved progress is the most faithful reproduction of the actual Granny meta-loop. The standout buildable idea is the low-pass-BiquadFilter-through-walls audio cue (8000Hz same-room dropping to ~600Hz through a wall) that makes the sound-first design legible — high payoff, bounded cost. The signature under-bed held-breath beat is front and center. Nothing here forces a feature that the client has historically seen break.

Approach 2 (total 24): The 8-minute dawn timer reliably manufactures a crescendo and the speed-vs-noise decision is genuinely fractal, but it bites off ~2.5x the scope: THREE parallel escape chains, five scheduled global escalation beats, a full lights-fail -> blackout -> flashlight-rationing lighting transition, a 4-layer crossfaded procedural music engine, cross-run difficulty tiering, AND a SECOND roaming AI threat at T+7:00. Each of the last three is its own subsystem and its own failure mode. High risk of shipping all of it half-polished — exactly the outcome the client is furious about. It is also the least faithful: it discards the days-as-lives identity for a real-time clock.

Approach 3 (total 25): Highest fun ceiling (puppeteering Granny with lures, emergent physics stories) and faithful to the 5-day structure, but it stakes the whole game on the two hardest-to-ship features in cannon-es: (a) a navmesh that re-rasterizes when player-pushed furniture moves, and (b) emergent vertical traversal by stacking chairs/crates to climb. Dynamic-navmesh-on-moving-obstacles is the single most likely source of the precise pathing jank (Granny stuck in doorways, warping through furniture) the client explicitly rages about, and physics-stack-to-climb is the highest-variance reachability hazard. Its own failureModes section admits both. Too much of the design's value is gated behind features most likely to break.

Winner is Approach 1 because it maximizes the probability of a single focused build that actually runs at 60fps with no floating items and no pathing jank, while still delivering the genre's core tension and the most faithful Granny loop. The smart move is to graft the best legible-tension and crescendo ideas from 2 and 3 onto this safe spine.

Graft:
- From Approach 2: the 4-layer crossfaded procedural music as a 'tension HUD' (ambient drone / proximity swell / awareness-rate pulse / chase staccato). Even a stripped 2-3 layer version converts dread into legible information and is the single highest-fun-per-unit-effort add. Keep it data-driven off Granny's FSM state so it costs almost nothing once the FSM exists.
- From Approach 2: thrown-item noise lures (right-click throw, ~8 m/s, creates a NoiseEvent at impact away from the player). This is the most satisfying tension-RELEASE verb in the genre and is cheap given noise events already exist. Approach 1 lacks an active release valve beyond hiding — add this.
- From Approach 2/3: the multi-ray sight check (cast to head + chest + hips, require >=2 unobstructed) to prevent the single-ray-clips-a-doorframe false negative/positive. Approach 1's single-ray LOS will produce exactly that bug; adopt the 3-ray rule.
- From Approach 2: the diegetic timer framing technique applied to escalation — surface Granny's per-day speed/hearing ramp via in-world audio cues (breathing pitch, a distant clock) so escalation is FELT, not read off numbers.
- From Approach 3: the explicit surface-anchor + downward-raycast + overlap-reject placement algorithm (raycast down from above a named anchor onto the surface collider, rest the item's lowest AABB point on the hit, reject if the ray also intersects another collider within the item's bounds, log a console warning on failure). This is the most rigorous spelled-out method for the client's no-floating/no-clipping requirement and should be the canonical placement routine.
- From Approach 3: the reachability validation rule (every grab point <=2.1m from floor on a walkable nav cell, or explicitly tool-gated). Bake this into the placement validator so no item is unreachable.
- From Approach 3: 'lures get wise' anti-cheese (a 3rd lure within ~10s is ignored, lures 70% less effective during full Chase) — graft this WITH the thrown-lure verb so the release valve can't be spammed into a press-to-win.
- From Approach 3: the optional edge-of-screen directional pulse toward Granny when she is <8m, as a toggleable accessibility aid so the sound-first design doesn't exclude players on laptop speakers or with hearing impairment.

Must fix:
- Physics determinism is non-negotiable for the client's #1 rage: fixed timestep 1/60 with an accumulator + render interpolation, enable CCD on the player capsule and all thrown/small dynamic bodies, min collider thickness >=0.1m, clamp all body velocities (cap ~12-20 m/s), high contactEquationStiffness so the dresser doesn't sink, and sleep settled furniture to kill idle jitter. Spell this out as fixed config constants.
- Single-route design creates a single point of softlock failure. Add a defensive guarantee that the one chain is always solvable: validate every item/lock/code spot at world-gen (collision + reachability), and if any held key item ends up out of reach (e.g., shoved into a gap), teleport it back to its last valid rest spot after ~20s.
- The low-pass-through-walls audio cue is load-bearing for the whole sound-first thesis and must be tuned by ear, not just by spec: verify same-room (~8000Hz) vs through-wall (~600Hz) is unmistakable, that the 1/d falloff clamped to [1m..8m] localizes correctly, and keep the always-on 1Hz clock tick as a calibration anchor. If this is unreadable the entire core loop collapses to guesswork.
- Add an active tension-RELEASE verb. As written, the player's only release is hiding (blind + committed). Without the grafted thrown-lure, the squeeze never fully releases and the loop fatigues. This is the most important fun graft, not optional polish.
- Anti-camp/anti-cheese: implement the spec's own fixes — the random-investigate impulse (Day 4+) plus the 'standing still >40s with progress incomplete triggers a house creak that nudges Granny toward you' rule — or a single safe tile trivializes the game.
- Final-door alarm fairness: the 10s escape-alarm summons Granny from her CURRENT position (never pre-spawned between player and threshold), the porch threshold sits <8m from the door, and the optional shotgun stun is reachable as explicit insurance. An unwinnable alarm from the wrong spot is a guaranteed unfair loss.
- Granny LOS must use the multi-ray (head/chest/hips, >=2 of 3) check and all static wall colliders must be watertight (weld or overlap panels by ~2cm) so a 1px gap never lets her see through a wall and a doorframe never hides a visible player.
- Respawn clarity: on every knockout show 'Day N of 5 — Locks X/3, Items: ...' so players understand a catch costs a day but preserves progress. Misunderstanding persistence here makes players quit; the meta-arc only works if it's explicit.
- Granny pathing must be built on a navmesh of the STATIC floor plan only (furniture excluded from navmesh but present as physics colliders), with local steering around dynamic furniture and a stuck-detection fallback (velocity <0.1 m/s for 1.5s -> recompute, last-resort teleport ONLY off-screen behind a wall). Do NOT let barricades feed back into the navmesh — preserve this simplicity that Approach 3 sacrifices.
- Barricade balance: a single-furniture barricade must yield to escalating shove torque in ~6-9s (heavier stacks add only a few seconds) and shoving must emit a loud noise that reveals the player — a trade, never a permanent seal, or the chase breaks.

## Lens winner: Granny: Classic-Escape — Five Days to Get Out
Scores:
- Granny: Classic-Escape — Five Days to Get Out: fun=8 feas=9 faith=10 TOTAL=27
- Granny: Pressure-Cooker: fun=8 feas=6 faith=6 TOTAL=20
- Granny: The Long Night — Systemic-Sandbox Loop: fun=9 feas=5 faith=8 TOTAL=22

Rationale: Classic-Escape wins because its core structure IS Granny, and it is the most likely to ship working from a single autonomous build. The essence of Granny is a dread sandbox metered by 5 days-as-lives, knockout-respawn in the start bedroom with house progress preserved, and NO countdown clock — the grandma is the only pressure source, so pacing is emergent. This reproduces that 1:1 (perfect faithfulness). Its sound-first detection (low-pass cutoff dropping from 8000Hz same-room to ~600Hz through-wall, telling you whether a wall is between you) is the most authentic and teachable tension mechanic of the three, and it nails the genre's signature beat: the held-breath under-bed moment while her feet shuffle past at 0.6m and awareness ticks down. On feasibility it is the most contained — one primary route, a single Patrol/Search/Hunt FSM, deterministic placement in 3 collision-validated candidate slots per item, gradual awareness fill/decay instead of risky extra subsystems. That containment matters given an implementer who will get it wrong unless spelled out. Pressure-Cooker scores lowest: its hard 8-minute timer deviates fundamentally from Granny's feel (sandbox becomes speedrun) and it piles on the riskiest content to build (4-layer procedural dynamic music, a second AI creature, scripted blackout, flashlight-as-detection) — each a separate failure point. Long-Night has the highest fun ceiling (the five-honest-solutions-per-door fantasy) and is faithful, but carries the heaviest technical risk: navmesh re-rasterization on furniture movement, lure arbitration that must not become press-to-win, emergent furniture-stacking that must stay reachable without tunneling, and AI legibility amid dynamic obstacles — the hardest thing to land in a one-shot build, exactly the failure the furious client has seen before.

Graft:
- From Long-Night: make THROWN-ITEM NOISE LURES a first-class verb. Granny investigates the noise EVENT POSITION (where you were), not your current position, enabling misdirection. Cheap to add on the existing noise-event system. Anti-cheese guard: a lure only redirects if louder OR closer than her current target, lures ~70% less effective during active Hunt, and a 3rd lure within 10s is ignored.
- From Long-Night: Granny's predictable REST RHYTHM — returns to her bedroom to rock in a creaking chair ~12s every ~90s, telegraphed by an audio loop. A learnable, observation-rewarding safe window for the riskiest grabs. Predictability here is GOOD and deepens mastery with no extra AI complexity.
- From Pressure-Cooker: enforce a SQUEEZE -> RELEASE rhythm — Granny MUST fully de-escalate to Patrol with near-silent ambient between encounters, plus a ~3s post-chase cooldown where she searches but cannot instantly re-lock. Prevents noise-fatigue; makes encounters feel earned.
- From Pressure-Cooker: a SIMPLE 2-3 layer dynamic-music tension HUD (ambient drone -> proximity swell -> chase staccato) over the clock-tick anchor. Keep it minimal so it does not become a music-engine tar pit; converts dread-from-confusion into dread-from-information.
- From Pressure-Cooker: FLASHLIGHT RISK/REWARD (beam within Granny's FOV gives +30% awareness fill — she sees the light) plus battery rationing. Pairs perfectly with the dark basement/vent rungs.
- From both losers: ANTI-SOFTLOCK / reachability guarantees — at least one complete chain always solvable; teleport an unreachable dropped key back to its last valid rest spot after a timeout; downward-raycast-onto-surface placement with overlap reject and a load-time console warning on failure.
- From Long-Night: an OPTIONAL toggleable directional indicator (edge-of-screen pulse toward Granny when <8m) for accessibility and laptop speakers, so the sound-first design does not exclude hearing-impaired players or break on bad audio hardware.

Must fix:
- Granny must NOT be a memorizable fixed-node conga line. Add noise-driven Search detours, a periodic random-investigate impulse (Day 4+), and small stochastic patrol variation. Never visibly telegraph her next node. The #1 way the winner goes boring.
- Validate audio localization by EAR, not just code. Same-room (8000Hz) vs through-wall (~600Hz) must be unmistakable and the 1/d panner falloff must read correctly. Keep the always-on 1Hz clock tick as a calibration anchor. Unreadable localization collapses the sound-first game into guesswork.
- Detection must be gradual, never binary/instant-catch-on-sight. Enforce awareness fill+decay and the Search-before-Hunt ladder so being spotted is usually recoverable.
- Physics correctness is the client's stated rage and the literal ship-killer: cannon-es fixed timestep 1/60 with accumulator+interpolation, CCD on the player capsule and all thrown/small bodies, min collider thickness >=0.1m, collision margin ~0.04m, clamp max velocity (~12-20 m/s), high contact stiffness so the dresser does not sink, sleep settled furniture to kill idle jitter. Never spawn bodies overlapping — validate at load.
- Item placement must be deterministic AND collision-validated: raycast straight down from above each named surface anchor onto the surface collider, rest the item's lowest AABB point on the hit, and REJECT any anchor whose downward ray also intersects another collider within the item's bounds. Every grab point must be <=2.1m from floor on a walkable cell (standing reach) or explicitly tool-gated. Warn on any validation failure.
- Sight raycast must cast 3 rays (head, chest, hips) requiring >=2 unobstructed for a hit, and all static wall colliders must be watertight (weld/overlap panels by ~2cm) so a 1px gap never sees through a wall and a thin doorframe never hides an obvious player.
- Close cheese loopholes: no single safe tile that waits Granny out forever (random-investigate can route her anywhere; standing still >40s with progress incomplete triggers a house creak nudging her toward your wing), the wardrobe-peek gamble at awareness>60, and barricades that always yield in 6-9s of loud shoving (a trade, not an exploit).
- Make persistent progress legible on respawn ('Day 3 of 5 — Locks 1/3, Items: key, screwdriver') so players never believe a catch wiped unlocks. The single intended route also risks an ambiguous next rung — each item must give a one-line diegetic hint and codes must sit near their target (fridge magnets by the keypad).
- Guarantee the final-door alarm is always winnable: the 10s alarm summons Granny from her CURRENT position, the porch threshold is <8m from the door, and the optional shotgun stun is a clearly-routable insurance item — never arm an unwinnable 10s loss with her already between player and threshold.
- Granny AI must never visibly jank: build the navmesh on the STATIC floor plan only (furniture as physics colliders, not navmesh), do local steering/whisker avoidance around dynamic furniture, and if stuck (velocity <0.1 m/s for 1.5s while pathing) recompute or, last resort, teleport only OFF-SCREEN (verified outside the player frustum AND behind a wall).
- Granny must read as a hunched old woman, not a box: eye height ~1.45m hunched, believable procedural PBR materials with wear/grime/dust/irregularity, atmospheric fog + flashlight + real shadows. A blocky Granny fails the client outright regardless of systems quality.
- Make the escape a designed crescendo, not a fade-out: scripted final-second hand-swipe near-miss, door slam behind, then silence and an 'Escaped on Day N — caught X times' results screen that invites a lower-day retry.
